01 · Summary

The site explains who Northeast Arc is. It does not tell each visitor what to do next.

Northeast Arc serves three very different audiences from one homepage: families looking for help, donors and supporters, and job seekers. Today they all meet a nine slide carousel, a long services grid and a footer full of links. On phones the Donate button disappears entirely until the footer. parcels, the organisation's own social enterprise, is slide five of nine. The recommendations below give each audience a clear path and use the homepage's traffic to lift parcels sales.

The core problem

There is no single primary action. Nine rotating slides compete for attention, so families, donors and shoppers all have to hunt.

The biggest lever

On mobile, Donate is hidden in the menu and only visible in the footer, about 7,100px down the page.

The parcels link

The homepage reaches far more people than the store. A permanent parcels module would turn existing supporters into gift buyers.

02 · Strengths

What is already working

These are worth protecting in any redesign.

Human, credible story

Founded by parents in 1954, the origin story is moving and well written, and photos show real people served.

Social proof

Awards (Top Workplace, Top Women-Led Businesses) and partner logos (Cummings, BCBS Foundation, ECCF) build trust.

Clear intake contact

A referral email and phone number sit right above the services, so families have a direct line.

Accessibility intent

An accessibility widget and a language switcher are present on every page.

Well described services

Seven service areas each have a short, plain language summary and a Learn more link.

Strong impact numbers

10,000+ people, 190+ communities and 1,100+ employees are memorable proof of scale.

Finding 01
CriticalDonationEffort: S

On mobile, the Donate button is hidden until the footer, about 7,100px down

Why it costs you

Most nonprofit website traffic now comes from phones, often from social posts. If supporters cannot see how to give within a thumb's reach, many will not open a menu to look for it.

What we found
  • On desktop, Donate is a clear purple button in the header. On a 375px phone it is folded into the MENU.
  • The first Donate link visible without opening the menu is in the footer, about 7,100px down a 7,850px page.
  • The top 160px of the phone screen is used by social icons, Careers, News, Contact and a search box.
Recommendation
  • Keep a Donate button visible in the mobile header next to MENU, or add a slim sticky "Donate" bar at the bottom.
  • Move social icons and search into the menu on mobile to give the first screen back to content.
  • Use the same purple button style everywhere so Donate is instantly recognisable.
Expected impact if fixed
+40 to 80%mobile visits to the donation page
+15 to 25%online gifts from mobile

Finding 02
CriticalHomepageEffort: M

A nine slide auto carousel replaces a clear headline and primary action

Why it costs you

Carousels spread attention thin; most visitors only ever see the first slide. When that slide carries no message, the homepage's most valuable space says nothing.

What we found
  • The hero rotates nine slides: awards, jobs, parcels, legacy giving, a playground, mental health training and more.
  • On load, the first slide shows a photo with no headline or button for several seconds; on mobile the caption area is blank white.
  • The caption panel overlaps the slide indicators, and the "Read more" link is cut off by the decorative wave.
Recommendation
  • Replace the carousel with a static hero: "Lifelong support for people with disabilities and autism" plus three buttons: Get support, Donate, Shop parcels.
  • Move news, awards and events into a "What's happening" row of three cards below.
  • If a carousel stays, stop autoplay, show the caption on load and keep controls clear of the text.
Expected impact if fixed
+20 to 40%clicks from the homepage to key pages
-10 to 20%homepage bounce rate

Finding 03
HighparcelsEffort: S

parcels, the organisation's own store, is buried as slide 5 of 9 and a third level menu item

Why it costs you

ne-arc.org reaches families, staff, donors and partners who already care about the mission, the warmest possible audience for parcels. Today almost none of that attention is passed to the store.

What we found
  • The only homepage path to parcels is the fifth carousel slide ("Visit parcels at the Liberty Tree Mall") and About Us > Innovative Businesses > parcels.
  • The parcels page on ne-arc.org then links out with one "Shop Online Now" button.
  • There is no product, gift guide or holiday shopping message anywhere on the homepage in November.
Recommendation
  • Add a permanent "Shop with purpose" band on the homepage with 4 featured products pulled from parcelsgifts.com and a "Shop holiday gifts" button.
  • Add Shop parcels as a top level menu item (with an external link icon).
  • Promote parcels in the mailing list and on the Thank you page after a donation.
Expected impact if fixed
2 to 3xreferral visits to parcelsgifts.com
+10 to 20%parcels online orders from ne-arc.org visitors

Finding 04
HighDonationEffort: M

The donation page has no story, no impact framing and no "why give now"

Why it costs you

Donors give to outcomes, not to forms. A short story and concrete impact amounts typically raise both completion rate and average gift.

What we found
  • Apart from the embedded donation form, the page copy is a single link: "Click Here For More Ways To Give".
  • No headline, photo, impact examples ("$50 funds..."), monthly giving prompt or tax deductible note in the page content.
  • Header Donate and footer Donate point to different pages (/donate-online-to-northeast-arc/ vs /get-involved/donate/).
Recommendation
  • Add a headline and photo above the form, plus 3 impact tiers: $50, $150, $500 with what each achieves.
  • Default to monthly giving with a one time option; add "Your gift is tax deductible" and a charity rating badge if available.
  • Point every Donate link to the same page and add a parcels gift suggestion on the Thank you screen.
Expected impact if fixed
+10 to 25%donation form completion
+15 to 20%average online gift
+share of recurring donors

Finding 05
HighNavigationEffort: M

Families looking for help must decode service names; there is no "Get support" path

Why it costs you

A parent arriving after a diagnosis is often stressed and unfamiliar with the jargon. Guiding them by age or need shortens the path to an intake call.

What we found
  • Help seekers must know terms such as "Day Habilitation", "Specialty ABA" or "LTSS" to find the right service.
  • The referral email and phone appear as one line of text, easy to miss.
  • The services grid has 7 tiles in 3 columns, leaving Clinical Services alone with empty space on both sides; the grid is also coded twice (once per screen size), so screen readers hear every heading twice.
Recommendation
  • Add a "Find support for..." selector: Infant or toddler, School age child, Adult, Family or caregiver.
  • Turn the intake line into a prominent card with a "Request a call back" form.
  • Rebalance the grid (4 + 4 with a "Find support" tile) and remove the duplicate markup.
Expected impact if fixed
+15 to 30%intake enquiries from the website
Fastertime to first contact for families

Finding 06
MediumMobileEffort: S

Mobile first screen: utility bar and a blank caption area push content down

Why it costs you

Blank space during load looks broken, and very long pages hide the footer contact details and Donate link.

What we found
  • On first load the space below the hero photo is blank white until the slider script runs.
  • The page is about 7,850px tall on a phone, with services stacked as seven full screen tiles.
Recommendation
  • Render the caption in HTML on load instead of waiting for the slider script.
  • Show services as a compact two column icon grid on mobile.

Finding 07
MediumFooterEffort: S

Footer headings collide and text breaks mid word

Why it costs you

Small layout faults in the footer, where people look for contact details, chip away at the organisation's credibility.

What we found
  • The Headquarters and Quick Links headings touch, reading as one heading.
  • Narrow columns break "Massachusetts" and the email address across lines.
  • The upcoming event link reads "View Upcoming Event $" with a stray dollar sign.
Recommendation
  • Use a 4 column grid with fixed gutters; let long words wrap normally and keep the email on one line.
  • Fix the event link label.

Finding 08
MediumRetentionEffort: S

"Join our mailing list" sends people to another page instead of capturing the email

Why it costs you

Every extra click loses subscribers. The mailing list is the cheapest channel for donations, events and parcels promotions.

What we found
  • The large Sign up button opens /get-involved/ rather than showing an email field.
  • Impact numbers differ across properties: 10,000+ people and 190+ towns here, 12,000 people and nearly 200 communities on parcelsgifts.com.
Recommendation
  • Put an email field and button directly in the band; offer interests (Donor news, Family resources, parcels gifts).
  • Align impact numbers across ne-arc.org and parcelsgifts.com.
Expected impact if fixed
+30 to 50%mailing list signups

Finding 09
HighPerformance and accessEffort: M

A heavy homepage, and inner pages that refused our test browser

Why it costs you

Slow pages lose mobile visitors, and an over strict security rule can silently block donors and families, particularly on shared networks, VPNs or privacy browsers.

What we found
  • The homepage made 193 requests and transferred about 14 MB, with 156 script tags; full load took about 3.3 seconds on a fast connection.
  • After the homepage loaded, every inner page we opened in our test browser (About, Services, Donate) returned "403 Forbidden" from the server.
  • The same pages loaded when fetched by a different client, which suggests a firewall or bot rule that may also block some real visitors.
Recommendation
  • Audit plugins and scripts (events, Instagram, sliders, translation) and remove what is not used on the homepage.
  • Serve images as WebP at display size; enable caching and a CDN.
  • Ask the hosting provider to review WAF and rate limit rules and check server logs for legitimate visitors receiving 403 errors.
Expected impact if fixed
-50 to 70%homepage page weight
Recoveredvisitors currently blocked by 403 errors

06 · Method

How this audit was done

Scope and setup
  • Live review on November 4, 2026 of the homepage on desktop (1024 x 768) and mobile emulation (375 x 812) in Chromium.
  • The Donate and parcels pages were reviewed from their page source, because the test browser received 403 errors on inner pages.
Frameworks
  • Nielsen's 10 usability heuristics and a nonprofit donation journey checklist.
  • WCAG 2.2 spot checks (heading structure, duplicated content, tap targets).
  • Page weight and timing from the browser Performance API.
Scoring
  • Each area scored 0 to 100 against the checklist; the overall score is the average.
  • Severity: Critical blocks a primary goal (donating, getting support), High measurably reduces it, Medium and Low affect trust and polish.
Limitations
  • No access to analytics or donation data, so impact ranges are estimates.
  • Interior service pages could not be inspected visually; a follow up review is recommended once access rules are confirmed.
